# Reader test kit — not yet run

No participants are currently available. Automated checks and the team's walkthroughs are technical evidence, not evidence that new readers understand or enjoy the site. This kit is ready for a later study; no recruitment or outreach has been sent.

## Session

Allow 20–25 minutes per person. Start with three unfamiliar readers to find obvious problems; expand to five to seven if recruitment becomes possible. Include mobile readers and, where possible, someone who uses keyboard navigation or assistive technology. This is qualitative research, not a representative survey.

Ask permission to take anonymous notes. Recording is optional and requires separate consent. Avoid collecting names, email addresses, or sensitive information in the research notes. Say: “We are testing the site, not you. Please tell me what you expect to happen and what seems unclear.” Do not coach participants toward the intended controls.

## Tasks

1. From the homepage, find a story that interests you in a tradition you know little about. Explain why you chose it.
2. Read enough of the Persephone story to explain what changes between two versions. Find the evidence for one difference. What does the page establish, and what remains uncertain?
3. Find an object or place connected to your reading. Explain whether the connection is specific to this story or just to the broader tradition.
4. Save something you would like to revisit. Navigate away, then find it again.
5. Use a collection, map, or family tree to discover another entry. On mobile, try the same task without rotating your device.

After each task ask: “What, if anything, made that difficult?” At the end ask: “What felt distinctive? What reduced your trust? What would make you come back?” Avoid suggesting new features before hearing the reader's answer.

## Notes

For each task record: anonymous participant code, device/input method, success without help / success with help / unsuccessful, route taken, confusing labels, observed errors, and the participant's explanation in their own words. Record elapsed time only as context, not a speed score. Separate observation from interpretation.

Prioritize obstacles that prevent finding, reading, understanding evidence, or returning to saved material. Repeated requests for the same capability can justify a prototype; a single compliment or feature suggestion does not validate it. After repairs, repeat the affected task with a new reader.

## While recruitment is unavailable

Continue keyboard, small-screen, screen-reader semantics, broken-link, source-locator, and persistence checks. Ask a future editorial or subject specialist to review a bounded set of claims, especially living traditions and comparison tables. Do not invent participants, quotes, satisfaction scores, or study findings.
